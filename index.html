Biology Life

Photosynthesis

Plants use light energy to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose, releasing oxygen. It happens in the chloroplasts, using the green pigment chlorophyll.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Aerobic respiration

Cells release energy from glucose using oxygen, mostly in the mitochondria. It’s roughly photosynthesis in reverse, and the energy is stored as ATP.

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O

DNA

A double helix of two strands held together by base pairs: adenine with thymine, cytosine with guanine. The order of the bases carries the genetic code.

A–T · C–G

Chemistry Matter

The pH scale

pH measures how acidic or basic a solution is, from 0 to 14. Pure water is neutral at 7; each step down is ten times more acidic.

pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]

Conservation of mass

In a chemical reaction atoms are rearranged, never created or destroyed, so the total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants.

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Ideal gas law

Links the pressure, volume and temperature of a gas. Heat a sealed container and the pressure rises; squeeze it and it warms.

PV = nRT

Physics Forces

Newton’s second law

The net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration. Double the force and you double the acceleration.

F = ma

Ohm’s law

The current through a conductor is proportional to the voltage across it, for a fixed resistance.

V = IR

Mass–energy

Mass and energy are equivalent. A tiny amount of mass holds an enormous amount of energy, because c² is so large.

E = mc²

Space The universe

Why the sky is blue

Air molecules scatter short (blue) wavelengths of sunlight much more than long (red) ones. That’s Rayleigh scattering, and it’s also why sunsets look red.

The speed of light

Light in a vacuum travels at exactly 299,792,458 metres per second. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth.

c = 299,792,458 m/s

Seasons

Earth’s axis is tilted about 23.4°. As we orbit the Sun, each hemisphere takes turns leaning towards it, which brings summer, not being closer to the Sun.

Did you know?

A teaspoon of a neutron star would weigh billions of tonnes on Earth, because its matter is packed so tightly.
